# how it works

How it works

The same loop software teams use for infrastructure and code, applied to your ad accounts.

  1. 01

    Spec

    Write down the state you want: the campaign ideas, channels, budgets, targeting and creative brief.

    campaign.yaml
  2. 02

    Design

    A design arrives as a pull request proposal from AI, showing exactly what would be included in the campaign.

    pull request
  3. 03

    Review

    You and your team comment on each element, ask for updates and approve, with every decision recorded.

    approved · checks passed
  4. 04

    Apply

    Once the design is merged, mops brings the ad platforms in line with the config files and publishes the ads.

    > mops ads apply

# trusted results

Every change is analysed

The agency plots daily spend, impressions, clicks, conversions and CPC for each account, campaign group and campaign, with an optional marker for every change.

You can always see what contributes to the marketing performance and make evidence-based decisions.

# what mops manages

Five components of a mops account

  • Platforms

    Channels

    Google Ads, Meta Ads, email campaigns.

  • Targeting

    Audiences

    Lists, attributes and look-alikes.

  • Images and copy

    Assets

    Copy and images, generated or added by hand, versioned with the campaigns that use them.

  • LinkedIn Ads

    Campaigns

    Campaign groups, campaigns and their ads, with audiences, assets and budgets.

  • In the agency

    Reports

    Daily spend, impressions, clicks and conversions, read live from platforms for the dashboard.

# agents

You ask, AI creates

mops builds campaigns from your specs, and AI creates copy and assets. When a reviewer comments on an element of the campaign, AI rewrites its prompt and adds a version with new candidates.

All changes are checked against your tone of voice and other creative requirements.

# history

Your account history is your Git history

Every change is a reviewed commit with its reasons attached. To undo a change, revert it: the next apply brings the platforms back in line.
